A **deal** is one transaction. A listing, a purchase, a rental placement.
Everything else in the app hangs off one.

## Creating a deal

**Deals → New deal** walks you through it in a few steps: what kind of deal it
is, who the client is, which property it concerns, and which process to
follow.

You can stop partway. The wizard keeps a draft, and picking it up later
resumes where you left off. Drafts nobody returns to are cleaned up after
thirty days.

**Attaching a workflow is the step that matters.** A deal without one is a
record; a deal with one is a process the app can help you run. You can attach
one later, and you can attach more than one — a pre-listing tidy-up and the
sale itself can run side by side, at their own pace.

## How a deal gets its name

Deals name themselves, from the subject property's street and the client's
surname. _"1420 Pearl St · Bosart Sale"_ — or just _"Bosart Purchase"_ for a
buyer who has not settled on a house yet. It updates as the facts change: link a
property, promote a different one to subject, or change the client, and the
name follows.

You can also just type one. A name you type wins and is never overwritten — so
if the derived name is wrong, or you have a house style, set it and forget it.

A buyer's deal often has no subject property for a while, because there is
nothing under contract yet. Those are named after the client until one is.

## The tabs

Every deal has the same set across the top, and the same header — the client,
the state, the stage, and the counts.

| Tab            | What is on it                                                                 |
| -------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Overview**   | Where the deal is, what is blocking it, and what happened lately              |
| **Timeline**   | Every stage in order, expandable, with its requirements and tasks             |
| **Tasks**      | The checklist — see [Tasks](/help/tasks)                                      |
| **Dates**      | The chain of deadlines — see [Dates and deadlines](/help/dates-and-deadlines) |
| **People**     | Everybody on the deal and their part in it                                    |
| **Properties** | The subject property, and any others in play                                  |
| **Offers**     | What has been offered and where each one stands                               |
| **Documents**  | Files against the deal — see [Documents](/help/documents)                     |

The Offers tab hides itself on deal types that do not have offers, and appears
as soon as one is recorded.

## Reading the overview

The overview answers _"where is this and what is in the way"_. If more than
one workflow is running, you get a card per workflow, because two processes at
different points cannot honestly share one progress bar.

The **Advance stage** button appears only when exactly one workflow is
running. With two, there is no honest label for advancing _one of these_, so
you advance from the workflow's own card.
