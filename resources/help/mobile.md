## Put it on your home screen

The app works in a phone browser, but it is better added to your home screen:
it opens without the browser bars, it remembers where you were, and — the part
that matters — **it is the only way notifications can reach your phone**.

**On an iPhone or iPad.** Tap the Share button at the bottom of the screen,
scroll down, tap **Add to Home Screen**, then tap **Add**.

**On Android.** The app offers you a button. If you dismissed it, your
browser's menu has **Install app** or **Add to Home screen**.

You only do this once per device.

## Turning on push notifications

**Settings → Notifications**, then **Turn on for this device**. Your phone
will ask you to confirm — that prompt comes from the phone, not from us, and
it only appears once.

If you say no, we cannot ask again. You would have to allow notifications for
Goldieflow in your browser's own settings.

**On an iPhone, add the app to your home screen first.** Push does not work
from the browser tab, and the switch will tell you so rather than pretending.

### If you said yes and nothing arrives

Check your phone's own notification settings as well as the app's. It is
possible to allow notifications in the browser and block them for the app in
iOS Settings, and when that happens everything looks switched on from in here.
The Notifications screen says so when it can tell.

### What a push says

Deliberately little: what happened, and which property. Never a client's name,
never a number. A notification can be read off a locked screen by whoever is
holding the phone, so the useful detail waits until you open the app.

## When you have no signal

You can **read** — your work queue and today's deals, as they were when you
last had a connection. A banner says when that was, so you are never guessing
whether a task list is current.

You cannot **save** anything: completing a task, adding a note, advancing a
stage all need a connection. The app says so rather than accepting the tap and
queueing it, because a task completion that goes through two hours later can
send a client an email you did not know was waiting.

When the signal comes back the banner tells you, and everything works again.

## What will not change

The phone is not a cut-down version. The two screens that matter on a phone —
My Work, and the deal you are standing outside — are the two the app was laid
out around.
