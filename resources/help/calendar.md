**Calendar** in the sidebar shows the month with two different kinds of thing
on it: **events**, which are appointments with a time — a showing, an
inspection, a walkthrough — and **deadlines**, which are the dates from
[Dates and deadlines](/help/dates-and-deadlines) and are days rather than times.

They look different, and on a day that has both, the deadline is listed first.
That is deliberate: a deadline is the thing that has a consequence if it
passes, and an 11am showing is not.

## Adding an event

Press a day, or **New event**. An event needs a title and a start; everything
else is optional.

- **All day** turns off the times. Use it for anything that is a day rather
  than an appointment.
- **Deal** attaches the event to a deal, which is what puts it on that deal's
  record and lets you find it later from the deal rather than from the month.
- **Who's coming** lists everybody in your directory, not just your
  colleagues: an inspection has an inspector on it and a showing has a client.
  This is a shared calendar, not an invitation — nobody is asked to accept, and
  nothing is sent, so putting somebody on an event tells your team who will be
  there and tells them nothing at all.
- **Repeats** turns one event into a series. Daily, weekly, every two weeks,
  monthly.

## Changing a repeating event

Editing a repeating event changes the whole series, because the series is a
rule rather than a pile of copies. If one occurrence needs to move on its own,
the honest way to do it is to end the series and add the odd one separately —
which is more work, and is the reason it is worth thinking about the rule
before you make one.

## Subscribing from your own calendar

You almost certainly already live in Google Calendar, Apple Calendar or
Outlook, and a second calendar you have to remember to open is a calendar you
will stop opening.

**Subscribe** gives you a URL. Paste it into your calendar app's _subscribe to
a calendar_ option — not _import_, which takes a one-time copy that never
updates — and your deals' events and deadlines appear alongside everything
else, refreshing on their own.

Two things to know about it.

**It is read-only.** Changing something in Google Calendar changes nothing
here. The feed is a view of this product, not a two-way sync, and a change you
make in your own calendar will quietly disappear the next time it refreshes.

**The URL is the password.** There is no sign-in step — anyone holding that
link can read that calendar, which is exactly why it works in an app that has
never heard of us. So do not post it anywhere, and if it does get out, press
**Revoke**: the old URL stops working immediately and you can generate a new
one.

You can have one feed for everything, or a feed for a single deal — useful if
you want to share one deal's dates with somebody without giving them the rest
of your week.

The feed carries titles, days, times and locations — and deliberately nothing
else. No attendee lists, no notes, and no client names: it ends up stored on
somebody else's server, and the less of your business that lives there, the
better.

A location is usually a property address, and it is there on purpose: an entry
you have to open this app to act on defeats the point of subscribing. If an
event's location is something you would rather not have on Google's servers,
leave it off the event and it will not be in the feed.

## What the feed covers

Ninety days back and a year forward. Far enough back that last quarter's
closings are still findable, and far enough forward that a closing date set
today lands in your calendar rather than appearing later when you have
forgotten it was coming.
