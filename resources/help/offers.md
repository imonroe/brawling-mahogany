The Offers tab records what has been offered on a deal and where each offer
stands.

## What this is and is not

It is **your team's working record**, not the contract. The signed document
lives in your e-signature platform, and this app does not try to replace it.
What it holds is the shape of the negotiation, so that the dates and deadlines
that follow from an accepted offer have something to follow from.

## Recording one

**Add offer** takes the direction — received or made — the amount, the status,
and optionally a submitted date and an expiry.

You can record an offer that was accepted last week. Entering things after the
fact is normal and the form expects it.

## Statuses

| Status        | Means                         |
| ------------- | ----------------------------- |
| **Draft**     | Being prepared                |
| **Submitted** | With the other side           |
| **Countered** | Answered with different terms |
| **Accepted**  | Agreed                        |
| **Rejected**  | Turned down                   |
| **Withdrawn** | Pulled by whoever made it     |
| **Expired**   | Lapsed past its date          |

**Expiry is worked out, not stored.** An offer past its date shows as expired
without anybody marking it so.

## Countering

A counter is **a new offer**, not an edit of the one it answers. Both stay on
the record, so the negotiation reads as the sequence it was rather than as a
single number that changed a few times.

## Accepting

Accepting one offer **rejects every other live offer on the deal**, in the
same action.

That is not tidiness. A deal with two accepted offers has two answers to
"when does this close", and everything downstream — deadlines, contingency
dates, the client's status page — reads that chain. There can be one.

An offer that was already **withdrawn** is left alone, because withdrawing is
something the offeror did and rejecting is your team choosing somebody else.
Those are different facts and the record keeps them apart.
