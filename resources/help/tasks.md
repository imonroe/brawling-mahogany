Tasks are the individual things somebody has to do. Both practitioners who
shaped this product named the same gap in their existing tools: a list of what
is actually outstanding, per deal and per person. This is that.

## Where they come from

Most appear on their own. When you attach a workflow to a deal, every task in
the template becomes a real task on that deal, grouped under the stage it
belongs to, with its due date worked out from when the stage started.

You can add your own at any time, on the deal's Tasks tab or from **Add task**
in the deal header.

## Required tasks

A task marked **required** is one the `required tasks complete` requirement
counts. If a stage has that requirement, every required task on it must be
done before the deal can advance.

This is the difference between a checklist and a workflow, and it is worth
being deliberate about. Mark too many required and the deal jams on things
that did not matter; mark too few and it sails past things that did.

Tasks that are not required still appear, still get assigned, and still show
as overdue. They just do not stop the deal.

## Completing one

Tick the checkbox. That is the whole interaction, and it is deliberately not
buried in an edit form — completing a task is something you do dozens of times
a deal, and editing its wording is something you do once.

Completing records who did it and when. Ticking something already ticked
changes nothing and does not record it twice.

**Reopen** unticks it if you were wrong.

## Assignment and dates

A task can be assigned to anybody on the team. Assigned tasks are what
**My Work** shows.

Due dates are read in your team's own time zone, not the server's — so
"overdue" means overdue where you are, which is the only definition that is
any use.

Tasks show as **Overdue** past their date, **Due today**, or **Open**.
